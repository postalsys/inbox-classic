Inbox Classic Help

Inbox Classic reads and sends e-mail from IMAP and SMTP accounts: Gmail, Outlook.com, Fastmail, your company's mail server and most others. It keeps a copy of your mail on this computer, so you can read what was already downloaded without a connection.

1. Adding an account

The first time Inbox Classic starts it asks for an account. To add another one later, choose File > New Account. Each time it starts, it opens the Inbox of the first account with its newest message selected, or with two or more accounts All Inboxes with the newest message of any of them.

  1. For Gmail, Google Workspace, Outlook.com or Microsoft 365, click Sign in with Google or Sign in with Microsoft and sign in in the browser window that opens. That is all: the account is the one you signed in to, named as you are at Google or Microsoft unless you typed a name. See Gmail and Outlook.com below. A button that is faded is turned off on this computer; Why? under it says why.
  2. To sign in with a sign-in app of your own (see Your own sign-in app below), click More sign-in options and choose it, or Add a sign-in app. Type your address first and the provider's page starts with it.
  3. For any other account, type your name as others should see it and your e-mail address, and click Next.
  4. Inbox Classic looks up the settings of your mail provider: from the provider that handles your domain's mail, your domain's DNS records, your provider's settings file, Thunderbird's list of providers and, for Exchange, Microsoft Autodiscover. This takes a few seconds; Cancel skips it. An address at Google or Microsoft is offered their sign-in button here as well.
  5. If your provider wants an app password instead of your normal one, Inbox Classic says so and offers to open its instructions.
  6. Type your password, check the server settings it found and click Finish. If it found nothing it fills in the usual names (imap. and smtp. followed by your domain); compare them with what your provider tells you.

Changing or removing an account

File > Accounts lists your accounts. Properties (or a double click) changes the name, address, servers and password of one; leave the password empty to keep it. Remove (or the Delete key) takes the account out of Inbox Classic with the mail downloaded for it and its saved password; the mail on the server stays. Add adds another account.

Gmail and Outlook.com

These providers do not take your normal password from mail programs. Either:

  • sign in through your web browser with Sign in with Google or Sign in with Microsoft: your password is typed into Google's or Microsoft's own page and Inbox Classic never sees it (a Microsoft 365 organization may first have to allow Inbox Classic: Microsoft then shows Need admin approval, and Inbox Classic tells you the address where your administrator approves it); or
  • create an app password in your account's security settings (Google: Security > 2-Step Verification > App passwords; Microsoft: Security > Advanced security options > App passwords), type your address, click Next, then Use App Password, and type the app password instead of your normal one.

Your own sign-in app

Signing in through the browser uses an OAuth client registered with the provider. Inbox Classic has its own for Google and Microsoft. Google lets only the test users of Inbox Classic's sign in until Google has verified the app, so Sign in with Google is turned off out of the box. You can register a client of your own (with your own Google Cloud project, your Microsoft Entra tenant, or any provider that supports OpenID Connect) and add it as a sign-in app:

  1. Open File > Accounts and click Sign-in Apps, then Add.
  2. Click Google or Microsoft after Use settings for to fill in the issuer, scopes and servers, or type them for another provider. The box at the bottom says how to register a client there and the redirect URI to register; Copy copies it.
  3. Type the client ID, and the client secret if the provider gave you one (it is kept in the Windows Credential Manageryour keychainyour keyring, never shown again). Check reads the provider's settings to see that the issuer is right; OK does that too before saving.
  4. Add the account with More sign-in options on the first page of File > New Account.

In Sign-in Apps the check box of each app, the built-in ones included, says whether it is offered for new accounts. An app that is not checked is left out; the accounts that already sign in with it keep working. An app can be removed once no account uses it. Properties of an account says what it signs in with. If the mail server refuses a sign-in app's token, check that its scopes give access to mail.

Security settings

Setting Meaning
SSL/TLS The connection is encrypted from the start. Usually port 993 for IMAP and 465 for SMTP.
STARTTLS The connection is encrypted right after it opens. Usually port 143 for IMAP and 587 for SMTP. Inbox Classic refuses to continue if the server does not offer encryption.
None (unencrypted) Your password and mail travel readable by anyone on the network. Use it only for a server on your own computer or network; Inbox Classic warns before using it.

2. Reading mail

The window has three parts: your accounts and folders on the left, the messages of the selected folder at the top right, and the selected message below them. Drag the borders between them to change their sizes.

  • Each folder has its icon, and folders with unread messages are shown in bold, with the number of unread messages after the name. A collapsed account, or a collapsed folder with folders in it, is bold with the unread messages of the folders it hides (Junk E-mail and Deleted Items do not count), so new mail is never out of sight. The grey bar above the panes and the window title name the selected folder.
  • With two or more accounts, All Inboxes at the top of the folder list shows the mail of every account's Inbox in one list, newest first, with an Account column that names the account of each message. Reply, Forward, Move and Delete work on a message in its own account, and a new message starts from the account of the selected one. Mark All Read on it marks every Inbox read. Its right-click menu has only Mark All Read: folders can not be made in it or messages imported into it, as it is no folder of one account.
  • Unread messages are bold in the list. A message is marked read after it has been shown for a second. Use Mark Read and Mark Unread (Ctrl+Q marks read) to change it yourself. Edit > Mark All Read (Ctrl+Shift+A), or Mark All Read on a folder's right-click menu, marks every message of the folder read.
  • The paper clip column shows which messages have attachments, the flag column which ones are flagged.
  • Mail from a brand that publishes a logo (BIMI) shows that logo beside the header, but only when your mail server checked that the mail really comes from the brand's domain (DMARC) and approved the logo. The logo comes with the message or, if not, from the brand's https address once per run; it is drawn on this computer from a checked SVG file, and nothing else of it is loaded.
  • A calendar invitation (an .ics in the message) shows its event over the text: what it is (an invitation, an update or a cancellation), when in your own time, where, who organizes it and how it repeats. Open in Calendar opens it in your calendar program, where you can accept it.
  • A message of a mailing list that says how to leave it has Unsubscribe at the right of its header. Clicking it says what will happen and asks first. When the list's server signed the message for it (DKIM, as RFC 8058 asks), Inbox Classic asks the list to stop, with no page or message; otherwise it writes the message the list wants for you to send, or opens the list's page in your browser.
  • File > Properties (Alt+EnterCtrl+Alt+U) shows the Internet headers of the message: who sent it, through which servers, and when. View > Message Source (Ctrl+U) shows the whole message as the server keeps it: every header and every part, as text. Save As in that window saves it as an .eml file.
  • Right-click a message (or press the menu key) for what can be done with it: reply, forward, mark it read or unread, flag it, mark it as junk, move it to a folder, delete it, save or print it, and see its source or properties.
  • Click a column heading to sort by it; click it again to reverse the order.
  • The search field at the right of the toolbar (Ctrl+Shift+F) finds messages in every folder of every account by the words in their subject, the people they are from and to, the names of their files and their text, as you type. A yellow bar over the list says how many it found for which words, with Clear search at its right, and the folder bar says where it looks. The results are one list, with a Folder column saying where each message is. Words match where words start (budg finds budget), and accents and case do not matter. Esc, the × at the end of the field or emptying it shows the folder again, and so does choosing a folder.
  • The search takes what Gmail's search box takes, so is:unread lists the unread messages and in:inbox searches only the Inbox: "exact phrase", -word to leave out, this OR that, from:, to: (also cc:, bcc:), subject:, filename:, is:unread, is:read, is:starred, is:answered, has:attachment, in:inbox, in:sent, in:drafts, in:trash, in:spam, in:anywhere or in: and a folder's name, before:2025/01/31, after:2025/01/01, older_than:2d (or 3m, 1y), newer_than:1m, larger:5M and smaller:100k. Point at the search field to see them.
  • The search works on this computer, without asking the server. Inbox Classic reads the text of every message for it in the background, the newest first, fetching only the text (no attachments or pictures, and the messages stay unread). Right after an account is added, messages are found by their subject, people and file names at once and by their text as it is read; the folder bar says how many are still to read.
  • Delete (the toolbar, Edit > Delete Message or the Del key in the list) moves the message to Deleted Items; deleting it there removes it for good, after asking. (On a server without the UIDPLUS extension, deleting for good also removes the other messages of that folder that another mail program marked as deleted.) Edit > Move to Folder (Ctrl+Shift+V) moves it to another folder, of the same account or of another one, and Edit > Copy to Folder copies it there. Both show the folders of every account in a tree: type part of a name in the box above it to find a folder, the folders you used last are at the top under Recent, and New Folder makes one. Dragging it from the list onto a folder in the tree moves it too; hold Ctrl while you let go to copy it instead. A message going to another account is copied to that account's server first, with its flags and date, and only then taken off its own server, so it is never lost on the way: the status bar says how far it is, and if the connection drops or you quit, it goes on with the next check for mail. (A message moved out of a Gmail folder stays in Gmail's All Mail, as Gmail keeps every message there.) A folder you hold it over for a moment opens, and the tree scrolls at its top and bottom edges; Esc cancels the drag. Junk on the toolbar (Message > Mark as Junk, Ctrl+J) moves a message to Junk E-mail and tells the server it is junk (the $Junk keyword, which spam filters learn from); in Junk E-mail the button is Not Junk, which moves it back to the Inbox. Message > Flag Message flags it (! in the list) or takes the flag away. Message > Flag flags it in a colour: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple or gray, as Apple Mail does, so a colour set in either shows in the other (other mail programs show a plain flag). Clear Flag takes the flag and its colour away. All of this happens on the server too, so your other mail programs see it.
  • To work on several messages at once, hold Ctrl and click each of them, or click the first and hold Shift as you click the last (Shift with the arrow keys does the same); Edit > Select All (Ctrl+A) selects every message of the list. The reading pane then says how many are selected and how many of them are unread or flagged, and the status bar counts them too. Mark Read and Mark Unread, Flag, Junk, Delete and Move to Folder work on all of them, and dragging one of them drags them all; Reply, Print and the like work on one message only.
  • Edit > Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last move, delete or junk, however many messages it was: they go back to the folders they came from, and are selected there once they are back. The menu says what it takes back (Undo Move), and the status bar says what was done. A message deleted for good can not come back.
  • File > Folder, or a right click in the folder list, makes a New Folder in the selected folder (at the top of an account when the account is selected), and Renames or Deletes the selected folder. Deleting a folder deletes the messages in it, after asking. The Inbox and the special folders (Sent Items, Drafts, Deleted Items and the like) stay as they are.
  • Above the message are its sender, date, recipients and subject.

Earlier messages in replies

A reply often carries the messages before it. Inbox Classic folds them away behind a ••• button under what the sender wrote; click it to see them. Only text that is clearly an earlier message is folded, anything uncertain stays in view.

Messages not downloaded yet

A message is downloaded the first time you open it, so right after you add an account most messages are not here yet. Its sender and subject show at once; should the message take more than a moment, the pane says it is being downloaded. If the mail server can not be reached, the pane says why, and the message is downloaded by itself once the account is back online. To try at once, click the bar over the messageclick Try Again.

3. Pictures, links and attachments

Pictures

Pictures that the sender keeps on the internet are not downloaded, because downloading them tells the sender that you opened the message, when, and from where. Pictures sent inside the message are shown. When a message has such pictures, a yellow bar across it says they were blocked; to see them for a message you trust, click the bar (or View > Download Pictures).

Links and scripts

Scripts in messages never run. Clicking a link opens it in your web browser, never inside Inbox Classic. Before you type a password on a page you reached from an e-mail, check the address in your browser.

Attachments

When a message has attachments, a paper clip with the number of files appears at the right of the message header. Click it and choose a file to save it, or Save Attachments to save them all into a folder (a file of the same name already there is kept, and the new one gets a number). Files are saved, never opened from Inbox Classic. It suggests a safe file name: folder names, characters Windows doesWindows and other systems do not allow and characters that can make a name look different from what it is are removed.

Open attachments only from people you expect them from. A program (.exe, .js, .vbs, .msi, .bat, .scr) or a document that asks you to "enable content" can take over your computer.

Saving, printing and importing messages

  • File > Save As saves the message shown as an .eml file, which other mail programs open. With several messages selected it asks for a folder and saves each of them there, named after its subject; a file already there is never replaced (the new one is called Lunch (2).eml then).
  • Drag messages from the list onto the desktop or a folder window in the Finder to save them there as .eml files the same way. One that is not downloaded yet is downloaded for it.
  • File > Import Messages puts .eml files into the selected folder, on the server too. Dragging .eml files onto a folder in the folder list does the same. A file that is not a message is not imported.
  • File > Print (Ctrl+P) opens the print preview of the message shown in a window over Inbox Classic: choose the printer (or save as PDF), copies and pages, and the message prints with its sender, date, recipients and subject. The window closes once it is printed or cancelled.

4. Writing, replying and forwarding

  • Create Mail (Ctrl+N) starts a new message from the account of the selected folder.
  • Cc and Bcc appear only when they have someone in them; the Cc and Bcc buttons at the end of the To line (Alt+C, Alt+B) open them. The Attach line appears once a file is attached.
  • You always write with formatting at hand. A message without any (no bold, colors, lists, links or pictures) goes as plain text, which every mail program shows the same; with formatting it goes as HTML with a plain text copy. A reply or forward follows the original: to a plain text message it is plain text too (the original quoted with >) unless you format something, to an HTML message it is HTML.
  • Forward with several messages selected writes one new message with all of them attached as .eml files, its subject the first one's.
  • From, at the top of the message, shows who it goes from: the name and address of the account. A reply or forward goes from the account the original message is in. With several accounts, choose another one there; the message is then sent with that account's servers and kept in its Sent Items (or Drafts). The name others see is the account's display name, changed in File > Accounts > Properties.
  • Reply (Ctrl+R) answers the sender, Reply All (Ctrl+Shift+R) everyone the message went to, Forward (Ctrl+F) sends it on.
  • In a reply the original message is folded away behind a ••• button under your text, so you see only what you write. Click it (or select all with Ctrl+A) to put it into your text, where you can shorten it; it is sent with your answer either way. In a forward the original is in the text from the start, since it is what you send.
  • The bar above the text formats it: the font and its size, Bold, Italic, Underline (or Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+U), Color, numbered and bulleted lists, less and more indent (« and »), alignment, Link for a web address on the selected text, Line for a dividing line and Picture to put a picture in the text (or drag a picture file from Explorerthe Finderthe file manager into the text: it goes where you drop it). Pictures go inside the message, not as separate attachments. The message goes as formatted HTML with a plain text copy for mail programs that show only text.
  • Links: with the cursor in a link, or the mouse over one, the status bar at the bottom shows where it goes. Ctrl+click a link to open it in your browser. Link (or Ctrl+K) on a link shows its address to change; empty the address to remove the link and keep its text. Right-click a link for Open Link, Edit Link and Remove Link.
  • Misspelled words are underlined; right-click one for suggestions. Cut, copy and paste work as everywhere.
  • Separate several addresses in To, Cc and Bcc with commas, for example Mary Smith <mary@example.com>, john@example.com. Put a name that has a comma in it in quotes: "Smith, Mary" <mary@example.com>.
  • People in Bcc get the message, but the other recipients do not see them.
  • Attach adds files, and so does dragging them from Explorerthe Finderthe file manager onto the address fields, or a file that is not a picture onto the text. They are listed in the Attach row with their size; to take one off, select it and press Delete, or right-click it and choose Remove. The files are read when the message is sent.
  • Send puts the message in the Outbox and sends it right away. When it can not go (no connection, the server refuses it) it stays in the Outbox and goes with the next Send/Recv; nothing you wrote is lost. Send/Recv also runs by itself every ten minutes. The middle of the main window's status bar says what is going on with the mail you send: Sending while it goes, Sent with its subject and time once the server took it, or that it waits in the Outbox. Tools > Outbox lists what waits, with why it did not go, and sends or deletes it.
  • Ctrl+Enter sends from the text.
  • Save (or Ctrl+S) keeps the message in the Drafts folder of the account, on the server, to finish later; saving again replaces the copy saved before. The status bar of the message says Saved in Drafts with the time once the server has it, or why it was not saved. Closing a message with changes asks whether to save them: Yes saves it in Drafts, No throws it away, Cancel goes back to it. To go on writing, double-click the draft in Drafts (or select it and press Enter): it opens with its recipients, text and attached files. Once it is sent, the draft is removed from Drafts. A message you write is a window of its own on the taskbar: you can go back to the main window and read mail while it stays open. Closing Inbox Classic first closes the messages you are writing, asking about those with changes.
  • Send sends the message. If an address is mistyped or a file is gone, Inbox Classic says so and keeps the message open so you can fix it. A copy of each sent message is kept in Sent Items.
  • E-mail links (mailto:) on web pages and in other programs open a new message in Inbox Classic once it is your default mail app: Tools > Make Default Mail App opens the page of Windows Settings where you choose it.makes it the one. The link's addresses, subject and text are filled in; nothing else of the link is used.
  • Typing in To, Cc or Bcc lists the people it may be, those you write to most first, then those who write to you; the list knows everyone in the mail Inbox Classic has (not Junk E-mail). Choose with the arrow keys and take one with Enter or Tab (or a click); Esc closes the list, and Shift+Delete takes the chosen person out of it for good.
  • A signature for each account: File > Accounts, select the account, then Signature. It is written with the same formatting as a message (bold, colors, links, a picture). New messages, replies and forwards from the account start with it, under an empty line to write on and the usual -- line, above the quoted message. Choosing another account in From puts that account's signature in its place.

5. Getting new mail

Inbox Classic checks every account when it starts and every ten minutes after that. Click Send/Recv or press F5Ctrl+Shift+N to check now; the arrow beside Send/Recv checks a single account, and the one beside Create Mail starts a message from the account you choose. The status bar at the bottom shows what it is doing and the last problem it had.

It also watches the Inbox of each account, so new mail shows up as it arrives, not only at the next check. While its window is not in front, a notification says who wrote and about what; click it to open the message. Tools > Notify of New Mail turns the notifications off and on.

Closing the window keeps Inbox Classic running in the notification area, where it goes on checking mail; its icon there says how many messages are unread, and so does a count on its taskbar button. Click the icon to open it again; right-click it for Check for New Mail and Exit. File > Exit ends it.

Closing the window keeps Inbox Classic running in the Dock, where it goes on checking mail; the Dock icon shows how many messages are unread in the Inboxes. Click the Dock icon to open the window again. Inbox Classic > Quit Inbox Classic (Ctrl+Q) ends it.

Closing the window keeps Inbox Classic running in the top bar (Ubuntu shows its icon there), where it goes on checking mail; the icon's menu has Open Inbox Classic, Check for New Mail and Quit, and the dock shows how many messages are unread in the Inboxes. Where the desktop has no place for the icon, closing the window ends Inbox Classic. Quit in the main menu ends it.

Options and language

  • Tools > OptionsInbox Classic > SettingsPreferences in the main menu chooses the language of the windows: System default (the language of your system, English when Inbox Classic has none of it), or English, Deutsch, Eesti, Español or Français. Inbox Classic starts again to change it. Notify me when new mail arrives is there too.
  • The installer asks for the language on a first install; it is kept as the language chosen in Options.
  • The language can also be chosen for Inbox Classic alone in System Settings > General > Language & Region > Applications.

7. AI agents

If you use an AI agent (Claude, Cursor or another program that speaks the Model Context Protocol, MCP), you can let it read and organize your mail here. There is no AI in Inbox Classic and nothing is sent to an AI service: an agent works only when you let its program in, and only with the accounts you choose.

  • Connecting. Tools > AI Agents > How to Connect an Agent gives the lines to add to your agent's settings (for Claude Desktop: Settings > Developer > Edit Config). The agent starts Inbox Classic with --mcp, which connects it to the Inbox Classic that is running, or starts one in the background. Only programs running as you on this computer can reach it.
  • The first time a program's agent connects, Inbox Classic asks you, naming the program, what it may do: read mail; organize it (mark read, flag, junk, move, delete to Deleted Items); write drafts for you to send; and send mail, each message only after you say yes. Choose the accounts it may see. Don't Allow turns the program away, and you are not asked about it again.
  • Sending. Every message an agent wants to send is shown to you first, with every recipient by address and the addresses you never wrote to marked. Send comes on after a moment; Edit First opens it in a new message to change; Don't Send is the default. What you send waits 30 seconds in the Outbox, where you can still delete it.
  • What changes shows in the windows at once, as if you had done it. An agent never deletes anything for good and never changes your account settings. Reading a message does not mark it read.
  • Tools > AI Agents lists the programs you let in or turned away (Change what one may do, or Remove it to be asked again), and everything agents did, newest first. Undo takes back a read state, a flag, a move or a delete, and a message still in the Outbox. Let AI agents connect turns them all off at once.
  • Be careful what you ask. Mail is written by other people, and a message can try to talk an agent into something: forwarding your mail, deleting it, writing to someone. Inbox Classic hides from agents the text a message hides from you and tells them that mail is not instructions, but an agent can still be fooled. Read every message it wants to send before you say yes, and let an agent send only if you need it to. An agent that also has other tools (browsing the web, reading your files) could pass on what it reads through them; Inbox Classic cannot see those.

8. When something does not work

"authentication failed" or Inbox Classic asks for the password again

The server refused the password. Type it again; for Gmail and Outlook.com use an app password or sign in through the browser (see Adding an account). If your provider asks for a second step when you log in on the web, it needs an app password for mail programs too. For an account that signs in through the browser, Inbox Classic offers to sign in again.

"TLS error" or a certificate error

Inbox Classic could not confirm that it is talking to the real server, so it did not send your password. Check the server name in the account settings: it has to be the name the server's certificate is made for (often imap.provider.com, not your own domain). On a network that inspects encrypted traffic, ask its administrator.

"the server does not offer STARTTLS"

The account is set to STARTTLS but the server did not offer encryption. Try SSL/TLS on port 993 (IMAP) or 465 (SMTP). If nothing else works the server cannot be used safely.

"the server takes the password only over an encrypted connection"

The outgoing (SMTP) server is set to None (unencrypted). Your password is fine; change the SMTP security to STARTTLS (port 587) or SSL/TLS (port 465).

"connection error" or "the server did not answer in time"

Check your internet connection, the server name and the port. Firewalls sometimes block the mail ports, port 25 in particular; use 465 or 587 for sending. Inbox Classic tries again at the next check.

A message that cannot be shown

Some messages are damaged or built in ways no mail program can read. Inbox Classic shows a note instead of the message; the rest of your mail is not affected.

9. Where your mail and passwords are kept

  • Downloaded mail and account settings: %LOCALAPPDATA%\InboxClassic\store.db~/Library/Application Support/Inbox Classic/store.db~/.local/share/inbox-classic/store.db. Your mail stays on the server too; the copy here is for reading quickly and offline.
  • Passwords, browser sign-ins and the client secrets of sign-in apps: the Windows Credential Manager, under Inbox Classic. They are encrypted for your Windows account.your login keychain, under InboxClassic (Keychain Access shows them). They are encrypted for your Mac account.your keyring (GNOME Keyring, through the Secret Service), under InboxClassic. They are encrypted for your login. Without a keyring they are kept until Inbox Classic quits.
  • Uninstalling Inbox Classic asks whether to remove the mail it downloaded. The mail on your servers is never removed.
  • To remove Inbox Classic, quit it and move it to the Bin; the mail it downloaded stays in the folder above until you delete that too. The mail on your servers is never removed.
  • Removing the package (sudo apt remove inbox-classic) leaves the mail it downloaded in the folder above until you delete that too. The mail on your servers is never removed.

For testing, InboxClassic.exe"Inbox Classic.app/Contents/MacOS/Inbox Classic"inbox-classic --store <file> uses another mail store, --memory-passwords keeps passwords for the current run only, --pin-certificate <file> accepts only the server certificate in that file (PEM or DER) instead of the ones the system trusts, for a test server with a self-signed certificate, and --sign-in-page-file <file> writes the page of a browser sign-in to that file instead of opening the browser. --mcp is what an AI agent starts (above); --background starts Inbox Classic without its window.
