Encrypted and signed mail

The bar above a message says whether it is signed by its sender and whether it was encrypted to you.

A valid signature confirms that the content has not changed since it was signed. To confirm who signed it, also verify who owns the key. An encrypted message can only be read with a matching private key.

The bar above the message gives the message as a whole:

ColorWhat it means
GreenSigned by the sender, with a key you verified.
BlueSigned by the sender with a key you have not verified, or encrypted to you.
AmberSomething is not right: the signature does not match, someone other than the sender signed, or a part could not be read.
GrayNot signed, or the signature could not be checked, for example because its key is missing.

A message whose parts are protected differently shows each part in a frame of its own, with its own label. The bar then counts the parts of each kind. Click a label, or on the bar, to see who signed each part, with which key and when.

Only the bar and the labels above the frames are written by Inbox Classic. A sender can draw anything inside their own message, including something that looks like a label. Look at the bar.

Pictures from the internet in an encrypted part load only when you click the pictures bar, every time. Its label then says that pictures were loaded.

When you reply or forward, only what was shown is quoted.

In the message list, a lock marks an encrypted message and a seal a signed one. Once Inbox Classic has decrypted an encrypted message, after downloading or opening it, the list shows its own subject and first words. Until then, the list and the new mail notification show .

Search finds a decrypted message by the words of its text, while is on under . AI agents never see what an encrypted message says, nor its files.
