The password is not accepted

What to do when the mail server refuses your password or sign-in.

You may see one of these:

  • In the status bar: “the server did not accept the sign-in (…)”
  • In the reading pane: “The mail server did not accept the account’s sign-in. The bar over the message list says why and how to sign in again.”
  • A window that asks you to enter the password of the account again

Inbox Classic asks once. After that, the account shows that it is not signed in until you fix it:

  • A warning mark on the account in the folder list (on its Inbox when it is your only account). Point at it to see why.
  • A bar over the account’s message list says its mail may be out of date, with a button to fix it.
  • The status bar says the account needs you to sign in, and the tray iconDock icon shows a warning.

While the account is signed out, Inbox Classic checks it less and less often (after 10 minutes, 30 minutes, an hour, then every 4 hours), so a wrong password does not get the mailbox locked. Click to try at once.

To fix it:

  1. Click the mark, or in the bar, and type the password again. You can also change it in the account’s properties in > .
  2. If your provider requires an app password for mail programs, create one in your account’s security settings and use it instead of your usual password.
  3. For Gmail, sign in through the browser, or use an app password if your account allows it and 2-Step Verification is on. For Outlook.com and Microsoft 365, sign in through the browser: an app password cannot replace browser sign-in for these accounts in Inbox Classic. See .

An account that signs in through the browser

When the provider ends the sign-in, Inbox Classic says “Sign-in to … was rejected or the session has expired.” It asks “” Choose and sign in once more in the browser. If you choose No, sign in later with the mark in the folder list, in the bar, > , or the account’s properties.

Providers end sign-ins for reasons of their own: Microsoft 365 organizations often require a new sign-in every few days or weeks, and a password change or an administrator ending your sessions does too. A sign-in unused for 90 days also ends.

  • If the bar says your organization’s administrator must allow Inbox Classic, signing in again does not help. Choose and send the link to your administrator.
  • If you signed in as another address than the account’s, choose and pick the right account on the provider’s page.
  • If the provider is down for a while, Inbox Classic waits and tries again; it does not ask you to sign in.
