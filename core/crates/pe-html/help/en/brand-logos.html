Brand logos

Some mail shows a provider-approved brand logo beside its message header.

Some companies publish a logo for their mail. Inbox Classic shows it beside the header of their messages. It does so only when your mail server confirmed that the message comes from the brand’s domain and approved the logo.

A logo indicates that your provider approved the sending domain and its logo. It does not verify the individual sender or the message’s claims. A message without one is not necessarily fake, as most senders publish no logo.

The logo comes with the message or, if not, from its secure web address. Downloaded logos are cached across restarts and refreshed when needed. Inbox Classic checks the picture before it shows it, and loads nothing else for it.

In the column layout, the brand’s logo is also in the message’s row in the list, and other rows show a contact’s picture when the sender’s domain is confirmed, or the sender’s initials. To turn the pictures off, turn off in . Inbox Classic reuses a cached logo across messages. An initial download or later refresh still contacts the logo’s server and can reveal your network address.
