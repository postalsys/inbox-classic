The connections Inbox Classic makes

Where Inbox Classic connects, and why, for the technically curious.

  • Your mail servers, over IMAP to read mail and SMTP to send it. Connections are encrypted with TLS, and each server’s certificate is checked against those your system trusts. Inbox Classic refuses a server without encryption, unless you choose and confirm.
  • When you add an account, to look up its servers. Inbox Classic reads your domain’s DNS records through DNS over HTTPS (Cloudflare, then Google), never plain DNS. It also tries your provider’s autoconfig file and Thunderbird’s list of providers (ISPDB). For Exchange, it asks Microsoft Autodiscover, which gets your password only when it asks for it over HTTPS. A server without encryption is never picked.
  • Browser sign-in, with OAuth 2.0 and PKCE, to Google, Microsoft or the provider of your sign-in app. The browser returns to Inbox Classic on this computer only.
  • Brand logos (BIMI), from the brand’s HTTPS address when the message does not carry a usable logo and no saved copy can be reused. Downloaded logos are cached across app restarts. This happens only when your mail server passed the message’s DMARC check and approved the logo.
  • Pictures from the internet, when you allow them, including through trusted-sender or automatic-download settings.
  • Unsubscribe, only with your approval. A one-click request (RFC 8058) requires a DKIM signature accepted by your mail server that covers both unsubscribe headers.
  • Encryption keys, when a recipient’s key is needed or you start a lookup. Address searches query the address’s domain (Web Key Directory), then keys.openpgp.org if enabled. Fingerprint or key-ID searches send that identifier to keys.openpgp.org instead of an address. Publishing your public key sends it to keys.openpgp.org and requests confirmation of its addresses.
  • Updates, from inboxclassic.com, to see whether a new version is out.

Sign-in codes are offered only when your provider’s Authentication-Results header (ARC-Authentication-Results at Microsoft) says the domain signed the message (DKIM). This needs no connection of its own.
