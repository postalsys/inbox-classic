Sign and encrypt a message

Sign a message to protect its integrity, and encrypt its content for you and its recipients.

A valid signature shows whether the signed content has changed. Recipients must also verify who owns the key. An encrypted message can be read only by its recipients and by you, and it is always signed too. For both, you need a key of your own.

If a recipient has no key

Encrypted sending requires a usable key for every recipient. If one does not, opens . There you can click to look again, to leave that person out, or when no content from encrypted mail remains. You can also ask them for their public key and import it.

Replies to encrypted mail

A reply to an encrypted message, or a forward of one, starts with on, and Inbox Classic asks before you turn it off. While the message contains text, pictures or attachments from encrypted mail, it can be sent only encrypted.

Drafts of an encrypted message are saved encrypted to your own key, without a digital signature. The sent message and its copy in remain encrypted and signed.

Share your public key

To let someone send you encrypted mail, choose > . Your public key is attached to the message as a file.
