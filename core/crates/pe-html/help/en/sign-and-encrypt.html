Sign and encrypt a message

Sign a message so its recipients know it is from you, and encrypt it so only they can read it.

A signed message shows that you wrote it and that nobody changed it on the way. An encrypted message can be read only by its recipients and by you, and it is always signed too. For both, you need a key of your own.

If a recipient has no key

An encrypted message is sent only when every recipient has a key. If one does not, opens . There you can click to look again, to leave that person out, or . You can also ask them for their public key and import it.

Replies to encrypted mail

A reply to an encrypted message, or a forward of one, starts with on, and Inbox Classic asks before you turn it off. While the message still quotes the encrypted text or carries its attachments, it can be sent only encrypted.

Drafts of an encrypted message are saved encrypted, and so is its copy in .

To let someone send you encrypted mail, choose > . Your public key is attached to the message as a file.
