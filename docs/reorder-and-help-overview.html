Part 0: a message's title shown as its first line

A Stripe payout notice (~/Downloads/payout.eml) has <title>Your €1,910.91 payout for EmailEngine is on the way</title> in its head, and the reading pane shows that sentence above the message. Other mail clients do not.

Cause. mail_builder() (pe-html/src/lib.rs) drops the title tag but ammonia keeps its text, as only script and style are cleaned with their content, and we take style out to keep style elements: <head><title>TITLE TEXT</title><style>p{}</style></head><body><p>body</p> comes out as TITLE TEXT<style>p{}</style><p>body</p>.
  1. .add_clean_content_tags(["title", "template", "noembed", "noframes"]): content browsers never show. None is an allowed tag (ammonia panics on a tag in both sets); style stays as it is.
  2. Check the other users of mail_builder(): the editor's document, sending (outgoing_html) and the plain-or-not comparison in editor.rs. A title has no place in a body we send.
  3. Tests in pe-html: a full document with a title renders without its text, body and style elements kept; the same for the other three.
  4. cargo test, clippy and fmt in core/; then payout.eml opened in the app.

Part 2: the help

What the review found

Wrong keys on the Mac today. The shared text writes Ctrl+Q for Mark as Read, which the Mac page turns into ⌘Q, Quit. Ctrl+F for Forward becomes ⌘F, Find. The new help writes no key by hand: each comes from commands.toml.
  • One page of about 880 lines; "Reading mail" alone is one list of about 140 lines (reading, search, conversations, delete, move, junk, flags, archive, folders, undo). The contents list has no links.
  • Internals inside the steps: Thunderbird's ISPDB, Autodiscover, UIDPLUS, $Junk, DKIM, RFC 8058.
  • Names unlike the app's ("Mark Read" for "Mark as Read", "Create Mail" and "New Message"), straight apostrophes in 38 lines, "colour", "grey", "Bin".
  • Missing or buried: Check for Updates, the Linux main menu, the spell-check language, conversation keys, what the app connects to.
  • Form: Tahoma at about 115 characters a line on every platform, no dark mode, no navigation or search; F1 always opens the top.

Style guide

  1. One task or one idea a topic, under about 300 words.
  2. A task starts with "To …", then numbered steps: one action each, then what happens.
  3. Sentences under 25 words; you, active, present tense.
  4. Controls in bold, by the app's own names (from the catalogs).
  5. Keys only from commands.toml.
  6. Protocols and RFCs only under Privacy and security or Advanced.
  7. The app's typography: ’ and “ ”, "cannot", US spelling, Trash on the Mac.
  8. Warnings in a note before the step.
  9. Tables for reference only.
  10. Up to three related topics at the end.

Order of work

  1. The title fix: one small PR, first.
  2. Account order: one PR, all three apps.
  3. Help engine: topics, keys and names from the sources, search, the export, the three viewers, context help, with today's text cut into topics.
  4. Help content: the rewrite, pictures, four translations, then screenshot reviews (MDN editor, Windows, Mac and GNOME help, four languages) until a pass is clean.
  5. Website help: the export on inboxclassic.com, after 3.
Before each commit: /simplify and /security-review on the change, with what they find fixed; prettier, cargo fmt and the tests. Then commit, push, open the pull request and watch CI until it passes.

Risks

Help Viewer on the Mac caches books and can be slow to pick up a new one; the build bumps the book's version. It can not be driven by the UI test, so the test checks the bundle and its index.
Mallard is a second output to keep right: the export is its only writer, validated with yelp-check in the Linux container.
A classic TreeView has no built-in reorder: the insert mark and auto-scroll while dragging are hand-made.
