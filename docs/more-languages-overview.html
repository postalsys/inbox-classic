Which languages next

After this round Inbox Classic speaks 24 languages: English, German, Estonian, Spanish, French and 19 drafted ones, all in Europe. The big gaps are outside Europe. This page suggests what to add next, in what order, and what each needs beyond the translation itself.

16 November 2026 · speaker numbers are rough, for scale only · the next language is now only catalogs, help and one line in pe_i18n::LANGUAGES, unless it needs one of the deeper changes below

Recommended order

Wave 1 · translation onlyBrazilian Portuguese, Turkish, Norwegian, Catalan, Serbian SLatin or Cyrillic script, everything already supports them. Days, then review.
Wave 2 · translation onlyIndonesian, Vietnamese, Malay, Filipino, Swahili SLarge and growing; Latin script; few plural forms.
Wave 3 · search and fonts firstJapanese, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Korean LSearch must find words without spaces, and the sign-in buttons need CJK fonts.
Wave 4 · right to left firstArabic, Hebrew, Persian LEvery window mirrored on three platforms, the editor and the documents too.

What the deeper changes are

Search for text without spaces

  • Search splits words on spaces (SQLite FTS5 unicode61): Chinese, Japanese and Thai would only find whole runs of text.
  • Add a trigram index beside it (FTS5's own trigram tokenizer) used for queries in those scripts, so any part of a word matches; a migration rebuilds the index in the background.
  • Needed for mail in those languages even if the windows stay English, so worth doing on its own.

Right to left

  • Mac and Linux mirror their windows by themselves; checking every custom view (the strip, the security bar, the tree's counts).
  • Windows: WS_EX_LAYOUTRTL on every window and dialog, the classic controls mirror, owner-drawn parts (list rows, toolbar) checked by hand.
  • The documents (reading pane, compose editor, help, print) with dir="rtl", and mixed text in subjects and names kept readable.
  • The biggest single piece: worth doing once for Arabic, Hebrew and Persian together.

Fonts

  • The Google and Microsoft sign-in buttons are pictures drawn per language: they need Noto Sans CJK and Noto Sans Arabic/Hebrew as the Greek and Cyrillic round needed Roboto's subsets.
  • The windows themselves use the system's fonts, which cover every script on all three platforms.

Quality, honestly

  • Maltese and Irish were skipped this round: the drafts would not have been a good start. The same care applies here: Malay, Filipino and Swahili most need a native reviewer.
  • Every draft ships marked for review, and the board post invites native reviewers per language.

Suggestion

  1. Wave 1 now, alongside the current work: Brazilian Portuguese, Turkish, Norwegian, Catalan and Serbian (Cyrillic). They cost what this round cost per language and reach about 300 million people.
  2. Wave 2 after: Indonesian and Vietnamese first.
  3. The CJK search index as its own piece of work (it helps everyone who receives mail in those languages), then Japanese, Chinese and Korean.
  4. Right to left as a planned feature, then Arabic, Hebrew and Persian together.
  5. Maltese and Irish when a native speaker offers to help (the board post can ask).
