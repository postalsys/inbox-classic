The settings window, on three platforms

A UX review of Tools > Options (Windows), Inbox Classic > Settings (Mac) and Preferences (Linux), and the plan to give all three the same three pages in each platform's own form.

Review of 2026-11-16. Plan: docs/settings-plan.md. Screenshots from the UI tests of 2026-11-15 (the Mac ones predate the last wording review).

Today

13 settings in one long column on every platform, in three different orders, with three sets of labels for the same things. None follows its platform's settings pattern.

Proposed

General, Reading, Downloading: the same pages, groups and order everywhere. A tabbed property sheet on Windows, toolbar panes on the Mac, an AdwPreferencesDialog with pages on Linux.

Cost

7 new strings, 5 merged ids carried over (6 ids removed), 10 strings gain an access key. One pull request, the order kept by a new core/settings.toml.

In the core, the help, the tests

core/settings.toml

Pages, groups, settings in order, with kinds, label ids, platforms and dependencies; generated into the three apps like commands.toml; each app builds settings in one exhaustive switch.

Help

<b data-settings="reading"> writes each platform's path to a page; options-language becomes the "Settings" topic with a section per page; 9 topics lose their triple path.

UI tests

Each step selects its page; codes disabled with notify off; Mac title and last pane; Windows Apply. Screenshots per page, refreshed, in English, German, Finnish and the pseudo-language.

Decisions for the product owner

  1. Three pages or four (Sending on its own). Recommended: three; a page of one control looks unfinished. Sending moves out when it gets a second option.
  2. Page names. Recommended: General, Reading, Downloading; "Storage" is the alternative for the third.
  3. Make Default Mail App in settings too. Recommended: yes, in General, and still in Tools.
  4. Apply on Windows. Recommended: yes, as Outlook Express's Options.
  5. Generate the structure from core/settings.toml, or hand-build it. Recommended: generate; it is what keeps the three orders together.
  6. One id for notify, language and restart words on all three. Recommended: yes; Linux's "Notify of new mail" becomes "Notify me when new mail arrives".
  7. Mac: "Check for updates automatically" in General. Recommended: yes, later; bound to Sparkle, one new id.
